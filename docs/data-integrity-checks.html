Telemetry Integrity Checks

Ten signals that tell us whether the intranet numbers in Power BI can be trusted, and a check catalogue that would have caught the April identity change on day one.

ProposalSeptember 2026 · Analytics pipeline

What happened on 8 April

The browser identifier that ties page views to a person stopped persisting. Every page view arrived with a new browser identity and therefore a new visit. Page view totals were correct, and unique visitors were unaffected because they are counted on the employee identifier, so nothing looked wrong at first glance.

Consequence. Visits inflated, and pages per visit and time on page collapsed to their floor. Unique visitors stayed correct: they are counted on the employee identifier and e-mail from the telemetry, matched to HR data and transformed to a contact ID, not on the browser identity. The change was found by people weeks later, not by the pipeline. The vendor is investigating the cause; this page is about what we can do on our side regardless of the outcome.

The signal we missed

Absolute counts move with the business calendar. Ratios between fields do not, so they break sharply when the data model breaks. Two of them would have fired on the first day.

Page views per visit

Daily, page views ÷ visits. Expected corridor from the trailing eight weeks.

Browser identities per employee

Daily, distinct browser identities ÷ distinct employee identifiers, on views that carry an employee identifier.

Illustrative curves: the shape follows the observed incident (ratio 1.1–1.2 before, 1.0 after); exact values and corridor widths are to be calibrated on the stored data.

Four questions the pipeline asks every morning

Every check answers exactly one of them. Results are stored per day and per check, so an alert, the Power BI data-health page and the incident history all read the same table.

Did everything arrive?

Completeness and freshness of each layer.

  • Daily volume inside the same-weekday corridor Bronze
  • Newest event no older than 24 hours, per layer all layers
  • Every event recorded, none sampled away Staging
  • Row counts tie out from Staging to Gold to Power BI every hop

Catches: stalled exports, partial days, silent sampling, lost rows.

Do we still recognise people?

Identity and visit grouping, the family that broke in April.

  • Page views per visit stays at 1.1–1.2 Silver
  • Browser identities recur from day to day Silver
  • About one browser identity per employee and day Silver
  • Cookie-based visits agree with employee-based visits Silver

Would have caught 8 April on day one.

Does the data still look like the data?

Schema, formats and the tracking software itself.

  • Same columns and property keys as the contract Staging
  • Empty-value share per key field unchanged Bronze
  • Tracking-software version unchanged, or the day is annotated Staging
  • Employee IDs, tracking IDs and dates well-formed; pages exist in the inventory Silver

Catches: releases and configuration changes before they reach a KPI.

Do the numbers make sense?

Plausibility of the KPIs and agreement between independent sources.

  • Each headline KPI inside its corridor, no step change Gold
  • Gold visits and people equal a fresh recount from the raw events Gold
  • Top pages and tracking coverage stable week over week Gold
  • The number in Power BI equals the number in Gold Semantic

Catches: anything the first three missed, from the outside.

What happens when a check fails

Info

The day is annotated in the check table, for example when a new tracking-software version appears. Nothing else changes.

Warning

Reports keep refreshing. The affected KPI card and the data-health page show a badge, and the data owner is notified the same morning.

Blocker

The Gold and semantic refresh is held for the affected dates. Every report page shows "Data under review since …", and an incident is opened.

Robust by design, not only monitored

Unique visitors are already robust: the employee identifier and e-mail from the telemetry are matched to HR data and transformed to a contact ID, so the April change did not touch them. Visits are not: they count the cookie session identifier. The pipeline already derives a visit from the person plus a 30-minute inactivity rule. Recommendation: apply the same principle to visits, and keep the cookie-based counts as monitored signals.

Decision needed

The current standard counts visits on the cookie session identifier (set July 2026). Changing the definition changes reported history: either restate past figures or report both with a clear label. Thresholds on this page are starting points to be calibrated on at least eight weeks of pre-April data.
