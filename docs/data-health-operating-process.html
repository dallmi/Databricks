Data Health Operating Process

How the checks run once they are trusted, who looks at the results, and what happens when something new appears.

ProposalSeptember 2026 · Analytics pipeline

The rule the whole process rests on

Alert on change, never on failure

Eight checks have been failing every day since 7 April and will keep failing until the source is repaired. A process that alerts whenever a check fails would send those eight every morning, for months. Within a week nobody reads the mail, and the ninth alert, the one that matters, arrives into an inbox that has already been trained to ignore it.

So the daily job stays silent while the picture is unchanged. It speaks only when a cluster appears that was not there yesterday, when a known cluster moves, or when something recovers. All three are computed, not judged: a cluster is a set of checks that began failing on the same day, and movement is the seven-day mean against the prior twenty-eight.

8 permanent failures × 250 working days = 2,000 ignorable alerts a year, or 0 if the trigger is change

Who looks, at what, and when

Four audiences, four surfaces. Nobody is asked to read a dashboard daily out of goodwill, because nobody does.

Data engineer on dutynamed rota, one person at a time
An alert, only when something changed. Triage within one working day: either fix it, or record what it is. Recording the cause is what stops it alerting again, which is the incentive we want. What is different since yesterday?
Data teamweekly, fifteen minutes
The health board, every Monday. Catches slow drift that never trips a threshold, reviews the clusters still marked cause not yet identified, and retires the ones that have been resolved. What is drifting, and what is still open?
First and second lineon demand, no training needed
The health board, when somebody asks. Organised by published figure and written in plain words, so the answer to "is this number right today" takes under a minute and needs no knowledge of the checks. Can this person trust the figure they are looking at?
Report consumerspassive, always visible
A banner and a badge inside the report. Names the affected figures and, just as importantly, the ones that are still sound. They never open the board and never need to know it exists. Does this affect what I came here to look at?

When something new appears

The loop closes on itself: diagnosing a problem is what silences its alert, so the incentive points the right way.

A new cluster

Checks that began failing together on a day nobody has described. Described automatically from the measurements.

Duty engineer triages

One working day. The description already says what moved, since when, which figures it touches, and which slice of the estate deviates.

Cause recorded, or fixed

A row in the cause table turns it from unidentified into known, and it stops alerting. A fix makes it disappear on its own.

A cluster that nobody records keeps alerting. That is deliberate. An unexplained defect in the numbers a business runs on should be uncomfortable until somebody explains it.

Getting there

Nothing is created in production before the same thing has run in development and pre-production. The last step exists because a monitoring system needs its own settling period.

Development
2 weeks

Build against a copy. Replay March and April: the checks must flag 7 and 8 April, stay quiet on the days before, and raise nothing over Easter.

Pre-production
4 weeks

Run daily alongside the real pipeline. Calibrate thresholds on real volume and measure how often a check fires with nothing behind it.

Production, silent
4 weeks

Daily run, results stored, alerts to the data team only. Nothing appears in the report. This is where a noisy check gets found, before it has cost anyone's trust.

Production, live
ongoing

Banner and badges switched on in the report. Thresholds reviewed after the first month, then quarterly.
